A small model. An inspectable experiment.
Understand the controls, the model, and the limits of the evidence.
01 / SIMULATE
Physics keeps the score
Gravity, inertia, tilt and finite fuel determine the trajectory. Python integrates at 50 Hz, with one control decision per 0.2 simulated seconds. Three landing pads reward precise touchdown.
02 / DECIDE
Laya chooses typed actions
Telemetry and explicit guidance requests become two choice questions: rotation and thrust. The local MLX model returns option probabilities without generating text.
03 / EVALUATE
Separate model and guidance
Raw execution applies the model's answers. Assistance replaces every disagreement with deterministic guidance. A separately trained checkpoint learns guidance-following from labelled examples.
04 / REPLAY
Actual recorded decisions
This browser runs only VanillaJS visualization. It does not perform inference or simulate new flights. Download a recording to inspect every state transition and the exact prompt.
Reading the flight deck
Altitude is hull height relative to the selected pad, not clearance over local mountains. Negative vertical velocity means descending. Positive tilt is rightward. The path is recorded history, not a prediction. Probability bars are model preferences, not probabilities of safe landing.
Scope
An original Atari-inspired experiment, not ROM emulation or spacecraft flight software. No sound, coins, manual piloting or online training occurs in this application. Training is an optional, separate CUDA workflow; inference runs locally on MLX.
